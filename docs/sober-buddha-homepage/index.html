WHAT IS GROWING IN YOUR MENTAL GARDEN?

What we tend
is what takes root.

Anger, craving, fear… or concentration, clarity, and equanimity?

Anxiety, depression, and addiction can grow like poisonous weeds in the garden of your life. They are reinforced by habitual thoughts, emotions, and behaviors repeated without awareness.

To cultivate a healthier inner world, we learn to recognize the patterns that sustain suffering, understand the conditions that reinforce them, and intentionally cultivate new possibilities.

THE SOBER BUDDHA MANIFESTO

We reject the idea
that you are broken.

Clarity is a skill. Awareness is trainable. Freedom is experiential—not prescribed.

Anxiety, depression, and addiction are not personal failures. They involve patterns of thought, emotion, and behavior that can become deeply reinforced through repetition and lack of awareness.

We blend ancient wisdom from India with modern neuroscience, evolutionary psychology, and depth psychology to create meaningful opportunities for personal transformation.

We do not promise escape from suffering.
We promise understanding. Cultivate awareness. Uproot what no longer serves. Live awake.
AWAKENING ISN’T MYSTICAL. IT’S PRACTICAL

The mind can be trained—
just like the body.

Rather than suppressing symptoms or chasing short-term fixes, our approach builds skillful awareness of the mind’s changing nature.

Through direct experience and guided practice, learn to recognize how thoughts, emotions, and sensations arise, interact, and pass away.

QUESTIONS & CLARITY

Understanding
the approach.

Answers to common questions about mindfulness-based, existential, and Buddhist-informed counseling.

What is the biggest myth of meditation?+

Meditation is not about forcing the mind to become blank. It is a practice of developing awareness and observing experience.

What is the Law of Dependent Origination?+

Explore this question through the contemplative framework and resources shared by Sober Buddha Counseling.

Which is more effective: meditation or psychotherapy?+

Different approaches serve different purposes. A conversation can help clarify what kind of support may be appropriate for your circumstances.

Can this work alongside therapy or medication?+

Discuss your individual circumstances and current care with Michael and your existing healthcare professionals.

Is this approach religious or spiritual?

The approach draws from contemplative traditions while also engaging modern psychological perspectives. Contact Michael to discuss what this means in practice.

Do I need prior meditation experience?

You do not need to arrive as an experienced meditator. A complimentary conversation is a place to ask questions and explore the approach.
